### 2.1 Three families claim the same words as three other families (141 words)

These are not disagreements about what the compiler emitted. They are disagreements about **who owns the word**, and every one of them makes a naive sum of the eight documents' totals wrong. Summing the families as written gives 922 words for a 796-word program.

The first two are layering, not contradiction: descriptors sizes the specific mover at zero and figuratives/numeric size the whole unit, and at every site the two agree word for word (descriptors' 155-word and figuratives' 56-word headlines both reproduce exactly from the site tables). A design record must still pick one owner per word or it will double-count.

The third is a genuine ownership dispute, and it points both ways:

- **`TR( )` at LOC 01240–01252** is fully specified twice. Arithmetic §11 gives `W(A10) = 1 + prep + 1 + v + 1 + 3` = 11; compares §2.7 gives `TR = 1 + (comparison unit less its vector) + V + 4` = 11. Same eleven words, same decomposition, two homes.

### 2.2 Two incompatible rules for base-locator register liveness

Both are labelled pinned-at-the-diff, and they are not the same rule.

- **arithmetic §2:** "The sample reloads **at a label, at a section entry, and after any MOVPAK calling sequence**, and reuses the register inside a straight run."
- **compares §2.2:** "the generator caches what index registers 1 and 2 hold; the cache is cleared **at any word that carries a label and at any instruction that writes the register**."

They explain the same three reload sites (00555, 01301, 01405) by different mechanisms — compares reads 01301 as "the `AXT 6,1` at 01300 wrote IR1", arithmetic reads it as "a MOVPAK sequence intervened". No site in the sample separates them, because every MOVPAK sequence in the program ends in an `AXT` that writes IR1. A generator must pick one, and the pick is unforced.
