Review record · 2026-08-16 · chunk B2 · commit 1b6d804

Two rules one program cannot settle

Chunk B2 is the first verb generator: it fills the mnemonic, operand and OCTAL columns of every MOVE the 1962 payroll sample compiles, and every line it fills matches the listing byte for byte. Six rules carry it. Four are attested at several sites. Two are not — one statement attests each, and a rival formulation fits the same ink. Both are taken under the section 12 standing rule, and silence lets them stand.

What was decided, in four lines

  1. The CORRESPONDING emission order is level-major: pairs matched at a receiver's own level go first, receivers keep the clause's order, and within one receiver the matched groups emit in reverse description order. Statement 208 is the only statement that exercises the tie-break.
  2. A two-factor product loads the literal into the Q register, and where neither factor is a literal the right one goes there. Three sites attest it, and the sample has no product of two literals to separate this from the near-identical rival.
  3. Each rejected formulation reproduces the 1962 listing exactly as well. They part on inputs the one surviving program never contains, so no evidence can choose between them and no further scan work would help.
  4. Both are one commit to overturn, in lib/src/codegen/procedure.dart, and nothing outside that file depends on either.
Decided

1 · The order CORRESPONDING emits its pairs

The decision. The matched pairs sort on three keys, in this order: the depth of the receiving item below its record, then the receiver's position in the clause, then the matched group in reverse description order. Pairs inside one group keep their description order. _emissionOrder in procedure.dart implements it, and M4-9 item (d) records it.

Why the order matters at all. A base-register guard is emitted once and serves every later word that reads the same locator, and a MOVPAK dispatch kills the register cache. So the sequence of pairs decides how many guards the program contains. Get the order wrong and the word count changes, which moves every address after it.

The evidence for the level rule. Statement 221, MOVE CORRESPONDING MASTER TO BONDORDER, has one receiver, so it isolates depth from everything else. DATE and BONDENOMINATION sit at BONDORDER's own level and emit first, at LOC 01322 and 01324. EMPLOYEE.NUMBER and NAME, both inside a matched group, follow at 01332 and 01345. The last of them dispatches through MOVPAK at 01352, and any pair placed after that dispatch would have to reload its base register:

What statement 208 does settle. MOVE CORRESPONDING DETAIL TO PAYRECORD, CHECK is the only statement in the program with two receivers, and it settles that depth beats the clause's order. CHECK's EMPLOYEE.NUMBER is elementary at CHECK's own level, and it emits first, at LOC 00771, ahead of all five PAYRECORD pairs — though PAYRECORD is the first receiver written. The three CHECK pairs, all inside CHECK's DATE group, come last. A receiver-at-a-time expansion is therefore refuted: it would have finished PAYRECORD before touching CHECK.

The evidence for the tie-break, and its whole basis. PAYRECORD has two matched groups. The description writes EMPLOYEE.NUMBER first, holding DEPARTMENT and EMPLOYEE, then DATE, holding MONTH, DAY and YEAR. The listing emits DATE first, at LOC 01006 to 01025, and DEPARTMENT only at 01026. That one reversal is the entire evidence for the third sort key:

The full excerpt through LOC 01067, and both receiver descriptions, ship in evidence/.

What would mislead. Two groups is the smallest number that can show a reversal, and it is also too few to tell a reversal from several other orders. Sorting PAYRECORD's groups by descending target address gives the same answer here: DATE's targets sit at 00024 and 00025, and DEPARTMENT and EMPLOYEE both sit at 00020.

The rejected options.

A — sort the groups by descending target address
Cost: it fits both attested statements exactly as well, and it is a worse rule for a reader. Address order is an accident of how the receiver was laid out, so the rule would silently reorder a clause when a field moved in storage, and nothing in the listing would explain why. It also has no meaning across receivers, where one address is absolute and the other an offset from a base register, so it could never be the whole rule.

B — refuse every clause with two receivers
Generate nothing where the tie-break is load-bearing, under the M4-2 refusal doctrine. Cost: statement 208 is in the sample, so the sample would stop compiling and chunk B2 could not exist. The refusal doctrine covers shapes the sample never reaches; this shape is the sample.

C — the three-key sort (taken)
It is the smallest rule that reproduces the attested order, it states its own basis, and it invents no machine. To overturn: one commit rewriting _emissionOrder; the oracle catches any change that breaks statement 208 or 221. What overturns it: a second compiled listing with a three-group receiver, or a manual page describing the expansion. Neither is known to survive.

Decided

2 · Which factor of a product goes into the Q register

The decision. Where one factor is a literal, LDQ takes the literal and MPY takes the name. Where neither is a literal, LDQ takes the right factor and MPY the left. _product in procedure.dart implements it, and M4-9 item (e) records it.

The evidence. The sample compiles three two-factor products, and they cover three of the four arrangements:

Site 1 refutes the obvious rule outright. A generator that always put the left factor in LDQ would print LDQ 3)HOURS / MPY CP)+6 at LOC 00617, and the listing prints the opposite.

The rejected options.

A — MPY always takes a name
State the rule from the multiply's side: MPY takes a name wherever there is one, and the left name where both factors are names. Cost: none on any attested site — it agrees with the taken rule on all three, and on every arrangement except a product of two literals, where it says nothing at all and needs a tie-break of its own. It is the same rule read backwards, with one hole.

B — refuse the unattested arrangement
Generate only the three arrangements the sample prints, and refuse a product of two literals. Cost: it is defensible under M4-2 and it is what this chunk does for ten other shapes. It was rejected because the arrangement is not a shape: the same two words serve it, and the rule already has to answer which factor loads. A refusal here would mark a gap that does not exist.

C — the literal loads, else the right factor (taken)
One sentence, total on all four arrangements, and refuted by no site. To overturn: one commit in _product; the oracle re-checks all three sites. What overturns it: a compiled listing containing a product of two literals.

Settled

3 · What the chunk verified, and what needs nothing

The four other B2 rules are not underdetermined, and they are recorded in M4-9 without a decision behind them: the OCTAL column takes its operation codes from the emulator's own table, so the printed word and the executed word cannot diverge; a base-register guard sits at the word that uses its operand, attested by the guard for 1)BONDEDUCTION at LOC 00727 sitting between two words of a chain rather than ahead of it; a base load takes the lowest free register, attested by statement 208 taking XR2 because an earlier sentence left XR1 live; and ten legal shapes the sample never reaches refuse rather than generate invented text.
