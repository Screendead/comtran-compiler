Recommendation: align. ADD is the increment SET already performs, F p. 47 gives the shape, and the alignment reuses SET's attested chain words with no new form.

M6-9 bears on both calls, argued both ways

Your rule of 2026-09-14: “if it would compile in 1962, it should compile. If it wouldn't, it shouldn't.” (docs/design/m6-acceptance.md, M6-9.)

For align

  • M6-9's reading rule: a form F admits, that J does not contradict, and that 90.01 does not defer, compiled in 1962. F p. 42 states the alignment and F p. 43 gives three unequal-scale examples. J's MOVE sections say nothing on alignment (a search of the J 02 conversion for “decimal point” and “align” finds no MOVE rule). 90.01 lists no such restriction.
  • So a refusal is stricter than the 1962 processor, which is the case M6-9 forbids.
  • PR #143 settled the same question for comparisons the same way, with the same three words.

For refuse

  • M6-9 says whether a shape compiled, not which words it compiled to. The sample has no unequal-scale MOVE or ADD, so the 1962 words for these shapes are unknown. The evidence exists and is sealed until M7.
  • Our refusals are labelled “no sample instance”, not as 1962 diagnostics. A refusal states the gap honestly; it is not a claim that 1962 refused (M4-2 as amended).
  • The ADD rounding question shows that the reconstruction is not fully determined by the manuals.

The two readings meet on the sub-cases: align builds only where every word is attested, and refuses where the sequence is not.
